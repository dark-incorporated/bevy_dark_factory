## The owner's own questions, answered with numbers

**"I'm tempted to put a rule for no comments."** Measured over `crates/**/*.rs` at `0c74e140`: **462,249 lines, 98,590 comment lines (21.3%)**. Classified by pattern: invariants 12.1%, mechanism 7.8%, measured numbers 0.9% (together **20.8% load-bearing**), pointers 14.7%, history 7.2%, unmarked prose 50.7%. A separate 107-candidate restatement detector found **fewer than ten** comments that merely restate the line beneath them.

**Recommendation: do not adopt "no comments".** The half that reads as prose is largely continuations of a claim started above, not redundancy, and all six stale clusters (#543–#548) are *load-bearing* comments — the kind a blanket strip deletes exactly when it is most expensive. If a rule is wanted, the survey supports the narrower one: **a comment may not state a number or a name a test cannot check** — every cluster is that, and #549 turns it into a ratchet rather than a deletion.

**"Why does the gate keep dying?"** It never died on a test. Six attempts on 2026-11-22/23: three killed by the kernel's global OOM (the gate's `rustc` at 15.3 and 15.6 GB beside a CI job or the coding endpoint) and three by `systemd-oomd` on its slice swap rule ("memory used 33.1 GB of 33.5, swap 30.2 GB of 31.9"). Measured on the way: **one rustc peaks 3.7 GB at the dev profile's default codegen-units and 1.9 GB at one**. The passing attempt ran with one rustc at a time, the editor crate at one codegen unit, no no-swap wall, the coding endpoint stopped and the CI listeners paused. The build lens then measured the gate's real cost: **1,054 s for gate #10, of which the test step is 1,045 s and 463 s of that is compilation**; across 73 passing gates the test step is **94.8%** of stepped time and clippy 4.2%.
